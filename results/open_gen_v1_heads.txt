Generator variety, step 2 (31 Oct 2026): fakes from open models, made on Kaggle from the 792 pair captions.
Frozen DINOv3-L + linear head. Dev sets only; own threshold per head (at most 5% false alarms on every real
dev pool); app preprocessing.

== Data (scripts/generate_open_models.py, notebooks/kaggle_open_gen*.ipynb; lists: scripts/make_open_gen_lists.py) ==
SD 3.5 Medium (Stability AI Community License; MMDiT, 16-channel decoder): 396 images, 28 steps, ~55 s each on a T4
Kandinsky 2.2 (Apache 2.0; its own MoVQ decoder): 792 images, 30 steps, ~11 s each
PixArt-Sigma (OpenRAIL++): 396 images, ~23 s each. It uses SDXL's decoder (same VAE settings, checked), so it is a
  DEV TEST ONLY: training on it would make SDXL partly "seen".
All 1,584 images ok (no black fp16 images). Training: fakes of training pair captions, cropped and resized to their
real photo's size: SD 3.5 358, Kandinsky 715 (4 copies each). Dev: PixArt 396 (as generated) and the SD 3.5 /
Kandinsky fakes of dev pair captions (38 / 77, as generated; a known generator for F, new content).
F = E (results/generator_variety_v1.txt) + these 1,073 training fakes.

== Conclusions ==
-> The key finding: E, which never saw SD 3.5, catches only 34% of SD 3.5 images (dev captions, n = 38). A modern
   generator outside training can be missed badly: the risk for ChatGPT / Gemini / Midjourney at the judge test.
-> F (with SD 3.5 and Kandinsky): SD 3.5 66%, Kandinsky 100%, PixArt (unseen) 99%, Muse 94%; SDXL (unseen) slightly
   lower (90% vs. 92%; under the per-kind pools 79% vs. 86%). Fewer false alarms on real portraits (2.4% vs. 3.8%),
   no resolution shortcut, the darkness bias is small on these photos.
-> PixArt is easy for every head (95-99%), so as an "unseen" test it says little.
-> Choice rule (plan): false alarms first, then fakes per type, then the unseen generator -> F is the candidate,
   after my phone check (private test).
