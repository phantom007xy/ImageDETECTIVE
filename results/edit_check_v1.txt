Muse-edit check v1 on Meta's Perception Encoder (31 Oct 2026)

What: frozen PE-Core-L/14 (336 px, Apache 2.0, timm) reads the centre square of the photo; the mean patch token after
blocks 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 and of the last layer (6 x 1,024) feeds a linear head (src/models/pe_core.py,
src/models/train_edit_check.py). Why middle layers: results/backbone_probe_v1.txt.

Training (head_EC_both, weight decay 0.01): label 1 = rendered by Muse: 2,242 training edits of people-free Commons and
OpenImages photos + 707 Muse pair fakes; label 0 = real: the edits' originals, Commons train, SID-Set OpenImages photos
(files 10-29), pair reals (10,203). App view (centre square) + 2 random training views each: 39,456 views.

Calibration (python -m src.calibrate.calibrate_app, run by me with my private pools): the edit line is chosen JOINTLY
with head F: (head F says AI OR edit check says edited) at most 5% in every real pool, edit check alone at most 0.5%.

The line: set by ONE real photo, a heavily retouched professional studio portrait in the polished dev set (edit logit
+4.05; next real photo +3.44). Joint trade, measured: if head F gives up its top polished-dev flag (AI line -0.30 ->
-0.17), the edit line can drop to +3.44 with the same false alarms on every pool: edits v1 local 35 -> 41%, whole
76 -> 84%; v2 local 21 -> 25%, whole 62 -> 68%; Muse pairs 92 -> 95%; SD 3.5 66 -> 63%. Giving up more buys nothing.

Tried and dropped: three squares along the long side instead of the centre square (same or worse after the trade);
mean + spread (std) pooling (probe AUC 0.959 vs 0.972); anomaly-style scoring (chance).
