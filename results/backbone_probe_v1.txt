Which frozen backbone and layer sees Muse Image edits? (31 Oct 2026; python -m src.eval.backbone_probe)

Linear probe (logistic regression, src/models/linear_head.py) trained on the 2,242 Muse TRAINING edits (edit = 1, its
own real original = 0; people-free Commons and OpenImages photos, training prompts) and tested on held-out Muse TEST
edits vs. their own originals (different photos, different prompts): the hardest pairing, same content, only the
rendering differs. Same view for every model: the centre square at the model's input size, JPEG 90. Frozen weights.
Only Meta models are candidates for the app: DINOv3 ViT-L/16 (448 px) and PE-Core-L/14 (336 px, Apache 2.0, timm).

Test v2: 198 edits (132 local, 66 whole-image) of 99 new people-free OpenImages photos (SID-Set files 0-9, used nowhere
else) + their 198 originals. Weight decay 0.01 (0.001 and 0.1 give the same picture).

Test v1 (76 edits of 38 people-free Commons dev photos) agrees: DINOv3 last 0.786, DINOv3 all layers 0.918, PE-Core
all layers 0.955 (local 0.937, whole 0.991), anomaly-style 0.44-0.59.

Reading:
  1. The edit trace is there, but not in the last layer: middle layers (around block 12) keep how a picture was
     rendered, the last layer mostly what it shows. Matches RINE (ECCV 2024: intermediate CLIP blocks detect generated
     images better) and the PE paper ("the best visual embeddings are not at the output of the network").
  2. PE-Core (Meta) with all its layers is the best single model, also on local edits (0.96).
  3. Modelling only real photos (anomaly detection) does not work here: real photos are far too varied.
  4. Frozen PE beats the tuning runs (results/tuning_v1.txt: last-4 blocks reached 0.85 real vs. edits) and leaves
     head F untouched -> next: a separate Muse-edit check on PE-Core (src/models/train_edit_check.py).
  Caveat: an edits-only probe; the check still has to keep real photos (incl. my phone photos and selfies) under its
  line, survive WhatsApp copies and screenshots, and be measured together with head F (one false-alarm budget).
